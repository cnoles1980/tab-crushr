## Recommended positioning

Tab Crushr is the tab cleaner that gives people control over what closes and a satisfying reason to keep coming back.

The product story should combine two strengths:

1. **Confidence:** Tab Crushr finds likely clutter, but the user reviews every suggestion before anything closes.
2. **Momentum:** lifetime tabs, estimated RAM reclaimed, milestones, and post-crush feedback make cleanup feel rewarding.

Safety makes the product trustworthy enough to try. Progress makes it distinctive and habit-forming enough to remember.

## Maker's first comment

Hey Product Hunt — I built Tab Crushr because my tabs had become a pile of tiny open loops.

Most tab-cleanup tools seem to make you choose between doing everything manually and trusting automation to close the right things. I wanted something in the middle: let the extension find likely clutter, but let me review the decision.

Tab Crushr finds duplicate, related, stale, and superseded tabs across Chrome. You can:

- review every suggestion before anything closes
- close tabs or discard them to free memory while keeping them visible
- protect active, pinned, and audible tabs
- save a page for later without turning bookmarks into another junk drawer
- track lifetime tabs crushed, estimated RAM reclaimed, and milestones

The new progress view is the part I'm most curious about. My hope is that cleanup can feel satisfying without becoming pushy: you stay in control, but every small session visibly adds up.

Everything is stored locally in your browser, and the extension is free.

I'd especially love feedback on one question: does the progress loop make you more likely to clean up regularly, or would you want it to stay even quieter?

## Launch-day operating plan

### Before launch

- Confirm Chrome Web Store publicly shows version 0.1.3.
- Install the public build in a clean Chrome profile and complete one real crush session.
- Confirm the progress card, milestone state, and post-crush feedback appear correctly.
- Confirm the Chrome Web Store page, privacy-policy link, and support path load.
- Push the exact 0.1.3 source to GitHub so the repository matches the public package.
- Create the Product Hunt draft and preview every field and gallery image.
- Pick a day when Corey can reply to comments for most of the first 12 hours.

### Timing

For a small personal productivity extension, the recommended default is the first Saturday after the live-build smoke test, at 12:01 a.m. Pacific. Product Hunt reports that weekends can work well for side projects and personal apps and receive more Visit-button clicks, while 12:01 a.m. Pacific provides the full daily cycle.

If an existing audience or press moment is already building on a weekday, use that momentum instead. Readiness and authentic distribution matter more than a theoretical perfect day.

### During launch

- Publish the maker comment immediately.
- Share the launch without asking for upvotes.
- Reply to specific questions and criticism promptly and personally.
- Turn repeated questions into clearer Product Hunt replies, store copy, or roadmap notes.
- Capture Chrome Web Store install movement separately from Product Hunt votes.

### After launch

- Thank people who left substantive feedback.
- Categorize feedback into usability, trust/safety, progress motivation, and missing features.
- Compare Chrome Web Store users and installs before launch, after 24 hours, and after 7 days.
- Record what messaging drove comments and store visits.

## Success definition

Primary:

- 50 qualified new Chrome installs within seven days.
- 10 substantive comments or direct feedback conversations.
- At least three clear product insights worth testing.

Secondary:

- Product Hunt featuring or a daily ranking.
- New Chrome Web Store reviews.
- Evidence that the combined “safe + satisfying” message converts better than generic tab-management language.

Product of the Day is an outcome, not the operating goal. The launch succeeds if it produces users, useful feedback, and clearer positioning.
